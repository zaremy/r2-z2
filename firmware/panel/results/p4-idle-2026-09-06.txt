P4 — his idle timeout: INSTRUMENT-LIMITED, not answered
=======================================================
2026-09-06. 67 minutes with R2 left completely alone: the panel scanned for his
advertisement and never once connected, because our keepalive IS his wake
command and any session that connects has already destroyed what it wanted to
measure.

RESULT, SCOPED TO WHAT WAS ACTUALLY OBSERVED: eight sampled minutes across a
67-minute window -- t+0,1,2,3 (the validation run) and t+30,31,66,67 -- every
one of them showing 108-124 adverts. No sampled minute was silent, and the run
spans all four of the epic's checkpoints (5, 15, 30, 60 min).

A FIRST DRAFT OF THIS FILE SAID "advertised continuously for the whole 67
minutes, with no gap at any point". That is not what was measured. The board
logged every minute; I only CAPTURED eight of them, because reading the serial
line is a sampled operation and the rest scrolled past unrecorded. Eight
samples cannot rule out a gap in the other fifty-nine.

What the sampling does establish is that he was advertising at t+67 as
strongly as at t+0, so if he slept in between he was advertising again by the
end -- which makes a silent-while-asleep model harder, not impossible.

The fix for a future run is cheap and worth doing: report the CUMULATIVE
advert count, not a per-minute delta. A total of ~7,400 over 67 minutes would
bound the total silence to almost nothing from a single reading, where eight
per-minute samples bound nothing at all.

THIS DOES NOT MEAN HE STAYED AWAKE, and that was written down BEFORE the run
rather than after it. Two readings fit the data equally well:

  (a) he did not sleep within 67 minutes, or
  (b) he slept, and a sleeping droid keeps advertising.

Nothing here can separate them. The ONLY sleep this project has ever observed
was VISUAL -- CLAUDE.md, 2026-08-18: he "reverted to his resting alternation
and then faded out". Whether that state stops the advertisement has never been
established, so the advertisement is not a validated proxy for it.

THE INSTRUMENT ITSELF IS SOUND, which is worth separating from the result.
108-124 adverts/min is ~2/second, sensitive enough that a real stop would be
unmistakable and a single silent minute would be meaningful. It was validated
against a known positive before the run, which caught two earlier versions
reporting 1/min and 0/min -- both of which were a stale duplicate filter, and
the second of which read EXACTLY like a sleeping droid.

WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY SETTLE IT: the operator looking at him. The observable is
his lights fading, and it needs an eye. That makes P4 not "free" as the epic
estimated -- it is free of hardware time and costs operator attention, which is
the resource this project is actually short of.

CHEAPEST PATH: the operator is already needed at the droid for P1
(corner-to-corner touch calibration). If they glance at whether he is lit while
they are there, and again on a later pass, P4 gets its answer for no extra
trip. That is worth more than another hour of scanning.

CONSEQUENCE FOR D-023 AND CHILD 5: `released` still has no duration behind it.
AC2b already says it "renders provisionally until P4 lands", and it still does.
What has changed is that we now know a BLE scan cannot land it.

WORTH KEEPING ANYWAY: 67 minutes of uninterrupted advertisement is itself a
datum. Whatever he does when idle, he does not stop advertising for at least an
hour -- so a panel that treats "no advert" as "he is asleep" would be wrong for
at least that long, and any scan-based presence detection can rely on him being
findable across that window.
