P1 — corner-to-corner touch calibration: PARTIAL
================================================
2026-09-07. Operator touching the panel; coordinates read by direct CST816
register access (the path #104 proved), after two LVGL paths that never saw a
finger.

TOUCH WORKS. That was in doubt for three of the operator's trips and it was my
wiring, not the hardware, every time.

Best extremes observed, across two runs (a reflash resets the accumulator, so
these are not one continuous session):

  run A   12 points   x 130..314   y  88..322    gaps L130 R53  T88  B125
  run B    3 points   x 128..242   y 279..438    gaps L128 R125 T279 B9

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

  Establishes: the controller reports varying coordinates across a wide span of
  the panel, so the mapping tracks the finger. Combined with #104's 174 points
  this is no longer in question.

  Does NOT establish the EDGES, which is the whole point of P1. The best
  approach to any bezel is BOTTOM at 9 px (y=438 of 447). Left never got closer
  than 128, right than 53, top than 88. Those are middle-of-panel touches.

  So the open question stands exactly as #104 left it: a 1:1 map onto 0..367 is
  "reasonable and unverified" at the edges, and every edge-adjacent hit target
  in child 4 still depends on an unmeasured assumption.

WHAT IS STILL NEEDED: the four corners specifically, pressed hard INTO the
corner against the bezel, and a drag along each edge. A middle-of-panel touch
cannot answer it however many points it produces.

NOTE FOR THE NEXT ATTEMPT: the accumulator does not survive a reflash, and I
reflashed repeatedly while the operator was touching -- losing run A's data and
resetting to zero twice. Do not flash while a calibration is in progress.
